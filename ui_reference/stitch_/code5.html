format_quote Line 42

"The scholar who corrects texts is like a physician curing a disease; one errant stroke can alter the fate of an entire dynasty's recorded wisdom."

edit_note
Collation Note

In the Song Dynasty edition (circa 1102 AD), the character for 'stroke' (筆) was miswritten as 'bamboo' (竹). This alters the metaphor slightly, emphasizing the material rather than the action. Restored to original based on the earlier Tang manuscript fragment found in Dunhuang.

format_quote Line 87

"He observed the patterns of heaven above and the topography of earth below, synthesizing them into a continuous thread of history that binds past and present."

edit_note
Commentary

The phrase 'continuous thread' (一以貫之) is a direct allusion to Confucian philosophy. The juxtaposition here elevates the historian's role from mere recorder to a sage-like figure harmonizing cosmic and human affairs. The spacing in the original bamboo slips suggests a deliberate pause before this crucial phrase.

format_quote Line 112

"Words unwritten are like breath in winter; visible for a moment, then lost to the wind."

edit_note
Marginalia

A later addition found only in the Ming block-printed editions. It appears to be a poetic insertion by an unknown annotator rather than part of the primary text. Retained here for its historical significance in the text's transmission lineage.
